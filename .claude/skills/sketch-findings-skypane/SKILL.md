## Overall Direction

**Minimum supported viewport width: 360 px.** Developer decision, 2026-09-13, taken
on a real comparison rather than in the abstract. Measure and design down to 360 px;
**320 px is out of contract.** The two reference devices are an Android at 360 px and
an iPhone 12-16 at 390 px; 320 px is an iPhone SE 1st gen / iPhone 5, and also what a
390 px phone becomes under a large browser font-size setting — that accessibility case
is the one reason this floor is worth revisiting, and it should be revisited on a real
request, not pre-emptively.

What this does and does not license:

- It does **not** license shipping something broken at 360 px. That width is a real
  contract, and both fixes of 2026-09-13 (the Home recent-flight callsign, the État
  readings table) were driven by 360 px failures the phone-sized assumption had missed.
- It does **not** mean deleting the 320 px assertions that already exist in
  `companion/test_browser_ux.py`. They pass today, they cost nothing, and they catch
  real defects. Keep them.
- It **does** mean that if 320 px ever becomes the *only* thing blocking a design, the
  right move is to relax that width — not to contort the layout for it. The one known
  320 px imperfection today is cosmetic and accepted: the readings table's timestamp
  breaks mid-phrase (`08:00 (il y` / `a 44 j)`) instead of at the clean boundary it
  finds at 360 px (`31 juil. 08:00` / `(il y a 44 j)`).

Every value below was read live from `companion/static/style.css` (and `companion/contrast_check.py`) at execution time. This section is a summary with a pointer to the reference file that carries the full detail — do not treat it as the whole contract.

**Typography.**
- Four sizes: `--font-label-size` 14px, `--font-body-size` 16px, `--font-heading-size` 22px (was 20px SUPERSEDED, 06.6.4.1.1 D-10), `--font-page-title-size` 32px (was 30px SUPERSEDED, 06.6.4.1.1 D-10 — 06.6.2 D-15 had set it to 30px originally). Two weights: `--weight-regular` 400, `--weight-semibold` 600. Three families: `--font-ui` (system sans stack), `--font-mono`, `--font-serif` — now `"Iowan Old Style", Charter, "Palatino Linotype", Georgia, "Book Antiqua", serif` (06.6.4.1.1 D-08/UIR-20): the Georgia-first stack SUPERSEDED, so on a real Mac the headings now render in Iowan Old Style or Charter, not Georgia, wherever the OS has one installed.
- The page title (`.page-title`) now carries its own `font-weight: var(--weight-semibold)` and `letter-spacing: -0.015em` (06.6.4.1.1 D-10) — both override the shared serif rule's regular weight/`-0.01em` tracking. Section headings (`.text-heading`) stay regular weight at the new 22px. This, plus the nested-card-title tier below, is a deliberate three-rung ladder — semibold/regular/semibold — so all three read as distinct levels rather than one heading weight repeated at different sizes.
- `.site-title` (the "SkyPane" wordmark, sidebar + mobile header) is its own 24px serif semibold brand role with `-0.01em` tracking — a literal, not a resurrected `--font-display-size` (06.6.4.1.1 D-11); it is not a content-scale citizen and does not belong in the four-size scale above.

Body text, tables, form controls, nav links and monospace content stay on `--font-ui` — do not extend serif further. `legend` earned its place in this selector as a bug fix, not decoration: it is `<fieldset>`'s accessible group name and a genuine heading role, and being left out of the serif rule for one release let it render sans-semibold directly beside a serif-regular `<h2>` at the identical size.

**Motion (new, Phase 23, D3/CFG-32).** Two duration tokens, four `@keyframes` blocks, and a budget that is executable rather than prose.
- **`--motion-fast: 180ms` and `--motion-slow: 2s`** — the app's first new custom properties since Phase 19, and the whole of its motion duration vocabulary. Mode-independent, so they sit with the radii rather than with the themed colours. Two *categories*, not two speeds, and the test that sorts them is **"is anyone waiting on this?"** — `--motion-fast` is REACTION (the user caused it and is watching for confirmation: the Flights detail row and its chevron, the save bar's entrance and its counting label, selection scale, the wash fade, the preview crossfade, the dialog entrances, the switch track and thumb, the failure toast, and the fade on a region that refreshed itself — self-initiated, but somebody IS waiting to read the new value); `--motion-slow` is AMBIENT (the page runs it on its own with nobody waiting: the live indicator's breathing dot, the new-row arrival wash). **Live refresh (new, Phase 23, D1/D7/D14/CFG-34/CFG-35/CFG-37).** Four pages now refresh themselves from **one** loop (`companion/static/freshness.js`) driven by **one** Python registry, `layout.REFRESH_SWAP_SELECTORS_BY_PAGE`, selected by a page key `page_shell()` renders on `<body>` — the key is `nav_slug()`'s own value, so the key space has one definition site and a page cannot be given a key that disagrees with its route. **Review-feedback discipline (new, Phase 27, CFG-71) — a sixth standing contract, beside motion, colour, spacing, drawing and controls, because this phase's method is reusable and the phase exists precisely because it was missing.** Phase 27 opened on one diagnosed defect: the quiet-hours dial shipped with three individually correct, individually passing checks (the arc correct server-side, the handles proven to move, the value proven to persist) and nobody had asserted that the arc **agreed** with the handles after an interaction — two correct halves, the relationship between them unmeasured, and an arc that draws 23:00→07:00 beside fields reading 08:00→18:00 is worse than no arc at all. Three findings from closing this gap are recorded here as reusable method, not as history:
- **Assert relationships, not just endpoints.** The concrete, reusable pattern (`_assert_surfaces_agree()`, `companion/test_browser_ux.py`, 27-01): decode every one of N rendered surfaces describing one logical value to a canonical form (the unit the page's OWN surfaces already speak natively — here, minute-of-day, because three of the dial's four surfaces already publish it), build the SET of decoded values, and make three separate assertions, never one: (1) the set has exactly one member (the surfaces **agree**); (2) that member equals what the interaction **requested** (agreement on the wrong value is what a page that froze every surface together looks like from outside); (3) that member **differs** from what was there before the interaction (a no-op is the cheapest way to make every surface on a page agree, and agreement alone cannot distinguish it from a real change). All three clauses are required — dropping any one reopens a vacuity gap a mutation test will find.
- **A declared hit target is not a resolved one, and the cause is different every time — so measure the resolved box, in its own container, always.** Four independently measured instances now stand on record, across two phases, each defeated by a DIFFERENT mechanism: a quiet-hours dial handle at 43×43 (25-04, a `translateY`/rotate pixel-grid snap), the theme-carousel pagers at 30×45 (25-06, two adjacent `::before` synthesis zones colliding with each other), and `.copy-btn` at 34×26 in a Flights detail row (25-02's own finding, root-caused and fixed by 27-08: not the button's own declared values — which already resolved correctly everywhere else, including `.row-toggle` sharing the identical rule — but a ~4px gap to a trailing sibling crushing the downward reach, and a `overflow: hidden` clip boundary crushing the leftward one). The lesson is explicitly NOT "always add padding": it is "always measure the resolved box in its own container", because a rule that is correct in one container can still be defeated by that container's own neighbours or clipping, and the fix belongs wherever the actual cause measures, not reflexively on the control's own class.
- **A stale comment can sit beside correct code, indefinitely, because a string-comparison harness reads the code, never the prose beside it.** `QUIET_DIAL_RADIUS`'s own comment claimed "64 − 7 − 3 = 54" while the shipped radius was 78 (`176 // 2 − 14 // 2 − 3 = 78`) — found by 27-01 (the geometry decoder reads the CONSTANT, never the comment, which is exactly why the decoder never inherited the error) and corrected in the same commit that next touched the surrounding code (27-02, `2336074`, since fixing a comment nobody asked about is not this codebase's convention — a plan corrects a stale comment only when it is already editing the code beside it).
- **`git commit --only <path>` commits the pathspec's CURRENT WORKING-TREE content, not what was staged for it — a known sharp edge of this project's commit discipline, not a defect in whatever plan hits it.** Found by 27-08 while splitting one file's two independent CSS hunks across two task commits via `git apply --cached` on a partial patch: the partial-apply staged only one hunk, but the subsequent `git commit --only style.css ...` picked up the FULL working-tree file (both hunks) regardless, because `--only` reads the working tree at commit time, not the index state a partial `git apply --cached` prepared. No content was lost, duplicated or misattributed — only the commit BOUNDARY is imprecise when two hunks in one file are meant to land in two different commits. Disclosed in the SUMMARY that hit it rather than corrected via amend, per this project's "always a new commit" convention.
- **The Flights DESKTOP table's relative age does NOT tick, and the phone card's does — a Phase 23 finding, recorded rather than smoothed over.** `_when_cell_html()` composes its secondary line from `layout.relative_age_text()` directly (its own docstring says so, and it says why it does not reach for `concise_timestamp_html()`), so the desktop When cell renders a static age; the phone card goes through `concise_timestamp_html()` and therefore carries a live `<time data-relative>`. **This also corrects a stale entry of record:** 23-03's inventory routed this site to 23-08 while describing it as "the mobile card's relative-age secondary line" — it is the desktop cell, and it was not converted. Nothing blocks the conversion; it needs a plan that owns `history_page.py`. Full enumeration of every age that stays static is in `references/data-density.md` and in the Phase 23 coverage ledger.
