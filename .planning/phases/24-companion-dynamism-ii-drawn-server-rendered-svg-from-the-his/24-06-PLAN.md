<objective>
Home tells you what the frame will do next. It does not tell you what it has been
doing. D13 draws the day: a 24-hour band with a mark for every check-in and the
quiet-hours window shaded, so a glance answers "has it been waking normally?"
without opening Health.

Two things make this harder than it looks, and both are stated here rather than
discovered during execution:

1. **This is the phase's only time-domain drawing.** Every other one maps an index
   to an x position. A time axis is a different scale with a different failure mode
   — an hour with no data must still occupy its hour of width, which an index scale
   would silently compress away.
2. **360 px leaves about 330 px for 24 hours** — under 14 px per hour. At a 30-minute
   cadence that is 48 marks in 330 px: ~7 px apart, which is drawable. At a 60-second
   cadence it is 1 440 marks, which is not. The band must degrade honestly rather
   than drawing 1 440 overlapping ticks and letting the reader believe it shows
   them.

Output: a time-domain scale in `companion/draw.py`, a day band on Home, and a
caption that claims exactly what the band shows.
</objective>
