**Source:** Split of Phase 47.1 decided by the orchestrator on 2026-11-03 after the plan check (blocker B1 and warning W11: WAKE-03 and WAKE-04 depend on data that do not exist until the instrumented frame has run). Locked decisions are inherited from `47.1-CONTEXT.md`.

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Phase 47.1 builds the instrumentation, the server and model tooling, releases the instrumentation-only firmware (owner-gated) and records the baseline and the model verdict. This phase uses that baseline: it builds and measures awake-time or energy reductions of a redraw wake, chosen from the measured breakdown, and checks the model on the replacement pack (Soldered 333290, 10000 mAh, owned by Phase 47 until accepted).

Requirements: WAKE-03 (measured reduction of the energy of a redraw wake) and WAKE-04 (model checked on the replacement pack).

Depends on: Phase 47.1 (baseline recorded, tool committed). WAKE-04 additionally depends on Phase 47's acceptance and connection of the replacement pack.

Out of scope (as in 47.1): a new discharge study as such, solar or wall power, a different battery chemistry, companion UI beyond the existing Health data and export, anything that changes what the frame displays.
</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Inherited from 47.1-CONTEXT.md (locked)
- The owner is a French speaker and not an engineer; anything the owner does is plain French, step by step, with proper accents; repository documents stay in English.
- The multimeter-in-series measurement was refused and is never a dependency.
- Autonomy wins over freshness; six months between charges is the goal.
- Any firmware release is a field change to a battery frame: nothing is flashed, tagged, signed, scheduled, deployed or approved on the owner's behalf; releases are built in the pinned container and signed; the owner approves at each gate (signing, then production).
- No firmware release lands during an open measurement window without the owner's agreement. The reduction release is separate from the instrumentation release and comes after the baseline is recorded.
- Owner-reported fact (2026-11-03): a plane can pass every 30 seconds, so the image changes at almost every long-interval wake. Fewer redraws is a product and policy choice with a visible cost, put to the owner, not a defect to remove.
- Evidence rules: figures come from committed, tested tools or are labelled arithmetic or judgement; ratings and the rated curve are not measurements; the top of the curve (above 4000 mV) is not calibrated; a cost per wake is an energy estimate from voltage and timing, never a current measurement; owner-reported facts carry their date.
- The freeze list (`stub-server/measured_fields_freeze.json`), the hash-skip, quiet hours, display-off and battery-critical overrides stay unchanged by any reduction.

### Closure rules (agreed by the owner on 2026-11-03, owner-reported)
These rules replace success criteria that could not be reached with certainty inside the phase. The owner agreed them in chat on 2026-11-03.
- WAKE-03: each reduction the owner chooses is built, measured before and after under a claim rule fixed before the change, and recorded as demonstrated or not demonstrated; only demonstrated reductions stay on. A duration-only claim on device-reported seconds per redraw wake satisfies the requirement when the energy fit refuses.
- WAKE-04: the pack's curve and the predicted autonomy at the chosen interval are stated with their range once data exist; the phase may close with WAKE-04 recorded as pending data, with the reason.

### Owner decisions to put (from the drafts of the 47.1 plans)
1. Which candidate reductions are built (image compression, light sleep or lower CPU clock during the panel wait, cached Wi-Fi channel, and whether static IP is dropped or fully specified), and whether a duration-only comparison is accepted where the energy fit refused. Chosen from the measured breakdown.
2. The redraw-policy question: keep today's behaviour, a minimum spacing of M minutes between redraws (with the stated staleness and the replayed upper bound), or the owner's own definition of when a change matters. Any implementation is a separately planned change.
3. Bundled or one-at-a-time release of the chosen reductions (default one at a time, so each effect is attributable).
4. Go or no-go and date of the reduction release, each gate (signing, production) approved by the owner; on-glass check of the first refreshes by the owner.
5. For WAKE-04: when the replacement pack is connected, which interval it runs at, and when enough data exist.

### Owner idea of 2026-11-11 (owner-reported, in chat): separate the check interval from the redraw interval
Status: an idea to cost with the 47.1 data, not a decision and not a plan. It extends owner decision 2 above.
- Today one wake interval sets both how often the frame asks the server for news and, when the image changed, how often it redraws. A settings change (for example a switch to photo mode) is therefore only seen at the next wake, up to one interval later.
- The idea: the frame checks in often (for example every minute, or every few minutes), while the server keeps serving the same image for a chosen redraw period, so the frame's hash-skip leaves the panel alone until the period ends. Settings changes would then be picked up within one check interval.
- It is only worth building if a wake without redraw costs little. That cost is what the 47.1 baseline measures (awake seconds of a no-redraw wake; energy only through the model, with its range). The answer decides which check interval is affordable, if any; no figure for it exists today.
- Possible user-facing result, in the owner's words: an energy-saving feature where the owner chooses to refresh the frame only every X minutes or X hours, depending on how often flights pass. Orly, with a flight roughly every 30 s, would use a short redraw period or none; a quiet location could use a long one.
- Unchanged by any implementation: the hash-skip, quiet hours, display-off and battery-critical overrides, and the freeze list. Any implementation is planned separately, just in time, after the baseline.
