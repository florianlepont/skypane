## Phase Goal

**As a** person glancing at the frame on my wall, **I want to** see a real, airline-specific aircraft rendered in a poster-quality composition that stays legible on actual e-ink glass, **so that** the frame reads as ambient art I want on the wall rather than a data dump.

This plan delivers the slice the user asked for by name — *"l'illustration de l'avion et de son covering"*: the generic flat silhouette becomes a real, dithered, airline-liveried aircraft chosen per detected flight.

</objective>

<planner_findings id="F-03-resolution">
## Verified during planning: the alpha mask must be hard-thresholded, contrary to 03-RESEARCH.md Pattern 2

`03-RESEARCH.md` Pattern 2 states: *"Do NOT hard-threshold this mask to strictly binary... a soft alpha edge composited against a dithered region is fine since the dithered content itself is already constrained to the 6 legal indices."* **That reasoning is wrong and was disproved by direct execution against Pillow 12.3.0.**

Pasting a `"P"`-mode image into a `"P"`-mode canvas through an `"L"` mask does not alpha-blend colours — it blends the **palette index integers**. Measured: a source of index 1 (White) pasted over a canvas of index 4 (Blue) through a uniform mask value of 128 produced index **2** — Yellow. A gradient mask over the same pair produced indices `{1, 2, 3, 4}`, materialising both Yellow and Red. A binary mask over the identical inputs produced exactly `{1, 4}`.

Index blending is meaningless arithmetic on a palette image and is a direct source of the illegal colours the Colour contract and `_assert_palette_contract()` forbid. The existing `load_binary_mask()` already hard-thresholds for exactly this reason — its docstring frames it as an anti-aliasing concern, but the palette-blend hazard is the deeper one.

**Binding for this plan: the illustration's alpha channel is resized and then hard-thresholded at >127 to a strictly binary mask before `paste()`, exactly as `load_binary_mask()` does.** The cost is a hard 1px silhouette edge instead of a feathered one — which is what every other element on this panel already looks like, and matches Phase 2's real-glass "hard, flat edges" finding.
</planner_findings>
