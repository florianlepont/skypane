## Summary

The firmware wake path is already lean for a no-change wake: Phase 34 measured about 1.3 to 1.7 s total on the VPS (Wi-Fi about 1.15 s, of which about 1.0 s is the DHCP wait; TLS resumed in about 0.1 s) and 53 LAN no-change wakes at a median 1.26 s [CITED: hardware/PHASE34-HARDWARE-SESSION.md:96-103]. A wake that redraws is a different animal: the panel refresh is a fixed 31.5 s (31.50 to 31.67 s in 20 captured refresh wakes) and the 960,000-byte image download takes 3.1 to 17.9 s (median 5.9 s), for a total of 36.5 to 52.3 s [VERIFIED: tool, hardware/logs/phase34/*.log]. A one-mAh wake cannot happen in 1.6 s (it would need about 2.2 A), so the energy the old-pack tests attribute to a wake must be in the redraw wakes, not in the no-change path. The existing awake-time reductions (hash skip, TLS resume, keep-alive, DHCP restore, memtest off, panel rail only while drawing) are all already in the code.

The committed field data say something the CONTEXT does not: the gap between wakes minus `sleep_s` is NOT a clean proxy of awake time. At 60 s the excess is bimodal (1 to 2 s for no-change, 36 to 38 s for redraw); at 900 s it is 34 s (93 % of gaps in 30 to 39 s); at 3600 s it is 20 s (51 of 54 gaps in 15 to 24 s), which matches neither mode. A single hypothesis fits all three cadences: every long-interval wake redraws (about 37.5 s awake) and the deep-sleep timer runs about 0.45 % short, subtracting about 4 s at 900 s and about 16 s at 3600 s from the observed gap [VERIFIED: tool for the data; the hypothesis is my arithmetic, not established]. If true, "about 20 s over sleep at 3600 s" does not mean a 20 s wake, and the per-wake awake time is about the same at both cadences. This is the strongest argument for on-device `awake_ms` (WAKE-01): it is the only way to separate awake time from timer error.

Instrumentation is a small, well-contained change in four layers, none of which needs new libraries: a pure-C telemetry module (host-testable), one carry-forward struct in RTC memory written at the single sleep exit, one new versioned request header, and a new nullable column set on `device_health` with a matching tailer allowlist entry and export extension. The repo has no schema-migration mechanism (`init_schema` docstring says so); the safest additive route is `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN` guarded by `PRAGMA table_info`, with the read-only export tolerant of a not-yet-migrated database. The frozen field list (`stub-server/measured_fields_freeze.json`) states "nothing else a frame sends is parsed", so adding a parsed header needs the owner's agreement to amend it.

**Primary recommendation:** Ship in this order: (1) server-side storage and export of one new versioned `X-Wake-Stats` header, backward compatible, no frame change; (2) one instrumentation-only firmware release, scheduled by the owner after the 2026-11-07 bilan; (3) the model tool fitted on device-reported phases, refusing when unidentifiable; (4) only then awake-time cuts, chosen from the measured breakdown. Put to the owner early that WAKE-03 as worded targets the no-redraw path (about 1.5 s, at most about 1 s of headroom) while the evidence points at redraw wakes (download time, CPU idle during the 27 s panel wait).

### Wi-Fi connect (what is and is not optimised)

- Full driver bring-up on every wake: `esp_netif_init`, default netif create, `esp_wifi_init`, event handlers (`wifi.c:93-138`); full teardown on every sleep (`:234-265`). [VERIFIED: codebase]
- No scan tuning: the station config sets only SSID and password (`wifi.c:189-192`). No channel, no BSSID, no cached AP is used. Grep for `scan|channel|bssid|power_save` in `firmware/main/*.c` finds nothing relevant. The behaviour of the zero-initialised scan method is ESP-IDF default behaviour, not verified here [ASSUMED].
- DHCP: either static IP (only if provisioned: `wifi.c:181-187`, `apply_static_ip` `:42-78`; provisioned by `firmware/provision.sh --static-ip ...`, a USB step) or DHCP with `CONFIG_LWIP_DHCP_RESTORE_LAST_IP=y` and ARP check off (`sdkconfig.defaults:54-55`). Phase 34 measured that about 1.0 s of the 1.15 s `wifi_ms` is still the DHCP ACK wait, and the static-IP gain (about 0.7 s) is an estimate because H-10 was skipped [CITED: hardware/PHASE34-HARDWARE-SESSION.md:75-77, :136-141].
- Retries: on `WIFI_EVENT_STA_DISCONNECTED` the handler immediately calls `esp_wifi_connect()` up to 3 more times with no delay (`wifi.c:80-91`); the whole wait is bounded at 15 s (`state_machine.c:110`, `wifi.c:214-216`). Failure exits through `fail_and_sleep("wifi")` with exponential backoff 5 min base, 6 h cap (`backoff.c:4-16`).
- SNTP only if the clock is below 2020 (`wifi.c:148`), i.e. after a power loss; up to 10 s (`:153`). Not on a normal wake.
- Conditions on which awake time depends: Wi-Fi retry count (up to 4 attempts inside 15 s); SNTP after power loss; download throughput (3.1 to 17.9 s observed); panel guard wait (up to 90 s, only at fast cadences); OTA offer (UPDATING screen plus 1 MB download); enrolment. Captured Wi-Fi outliers: `wifi_ms` 5874 and 4059 in the Phase 34 captures [VERIFIED: tool].

- One TCP+TLS connection per wake, reused for setup, display and a same-origin image download (`api_client.c:49-65`, `:145-193`); a stale keep-alive is retried once (`:301-345`).

## 2. Telemetry that exists and how to extend it

### What the frame sends today
Four headers on every `/device/v1/display` request, plus one conditional: `X-Rssi` (`fp_wifi_rssi()`, 0 if unavailable), `X-Battery-Mv` (0 means unknown), `X-Fw-Version`, `X-Boot-Reason`, and `X-Ota-Result` only while an OTA outcome is pending (`api_client.c:246-268`; header hygiene `:72-81`). No timing, attempt count or boot counter is sent. The per-stage timing exists only as a console line (`app_main.c:151-159`, tag `fp_diag`, outside the Log Line Contract, `firmware/VENDOR.md:369`).

PROTOCOL.md is not in this repository; it is the upstream FlightPortrait document, referenced by `stub-server/VENDOR.md:517-539` ("Disagreements with PROTOCOL.md"). The contract in force is therefore the server code and the freeze list. The response keys are `image_url`, `image_hash`, `sleep_s`, `firmware`, `reset`, `led_enabled` (`stub-server/byos_server.py:1678-1710`); the frame ignores unknown response keys and header values it does not send are simply absent.

### How the server reads them and how they reach history
- Live frame, history path: the ONLY path is the Caddy access log. byos persists only the latest battery reading (`battery_state.json`, `byos_server.py:1643-1649`) and the firmware version/OTA result (`device_report.json`, `:1224-1225`); `X-Boot-Reason` and `X-Rssi` are only printed (`log_telemetry`, `:1517-1526`). `deploy/Caddyfile:20-45` documents this: the log is the "only durable per-request record", a JSON file `/opt/skypane/state/caddy-access.log`, 10 MiB x 5 rolled.
- The tailer `tail_caddy_battery_log` (`server/history_db.py:671-757`) keeps only lines whose `request.uri` is exactly `/device/v1/display` (`:718`), extracts headers through an explicit allowlist `_TELEMETRY_HEADER_ALLOWLIST = ("X-Fw-Version", "X-Boot-Reason", "X-Rssi", "X-Battery-Mv")` (`:84`, used `:724-727`) so a redaction change cannot leak a credential, converts `ts` to ISO seconds (`:737-746`), and returns readings. `apply_caddy_battery_log` inserts each with `INSERT OR IGNORE` (`:795-812`). It runs inside the 30 s poll oneshot (`server/poll_cycle.py:384-436`), only for the live device (`fleet_cycle.py:395`).
- Non-live (fleet) devices: byos writes a row itself from the request headers (`_record_device_health`, `byos_server.py:1103-1117`, called `:1677`).
- Schema: `device_health(id, ts, battery_mv INTEGER, fw_version, boot_reason, rssi TEXT, UNIQUE(ts, battery_mv))` (`history_db.py:127-138`). There is no schema version and no migration: the `init_schema` docstring says "a new table is free, a new column would have required inventing one" (`:102-106`), and `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` never adds a column to an existing table. The schema runs once per process per database identity (`:182-209`).
- Export: `GET /api/export/battery-history?since=` (`companion/export_api.py`), `history_db.export_device_health` opens the database `mode=ro` and selects five named columns (`history_db.py:483-508`); output is JSON Lines built from the fixed tuple `_COLUMNS` (`export_api.py:51`, `:105-108`). The test pins the exact key list (`companion/test_export_battery_history.py:22`). `hardware/logtools.py` reads rows with `row.get(...)` (`:767`, `:775`), so extra keys are tolerated by the existing consumer [VERIFIED: codebase].

### What a new field requires (checklist)
1. Frame: set the header in `telemetry_headers` and add its name to `clear_request_headers` (`api_client.c:72-81`) so it cannot leak onto the image request.
2. Caddy: nothing; Caddy logs every request header already (`Caddyfile:23-28`). Do not put data in the URI: the tailer needs the URI to equal `/device/v1/display` exactly (`history_db.py:718`).
3. Tailer: add the name to the allowlist (`:84`), parse with strict bounds, add keys to the reading dict (`:748-754`), pass them through `apply_caddy_battery_log` (`:802-810`) and `record_device_health` (`:368-374`, SQL `:71-74`).
4. byos fleet path: extend `_record_device_health` identically; use a strict regex parser like `parse_battery_mv` (`byos_server.py:776-796`).
5. Schema: see below.
6. Export: extend the SELECT, `_COLUMNS` and the export test.
7. Freeze list: `stub-server/measured_fields_freeze.json` (`telemetry_request_headers`: "nothing else a frame sends is parsed"; the file says it changes only with the owner's agreement) and its enforcing test `stub-server/test_phase47_freeze.py:249-264`. That test measures effects on `battery_state.json`, `device_report.json` and stdout, so a header parsed into history only would not fail the test, but it would contradict the file's stated intent. Get the owner's agreement and amend the file, rather than relying on the test not noticing.

### Recommended field set and encoding
One new header, versioned, key=value, about 80 bytes: `X-Wake-Stats: v=1;seq=<boot_count>;bt=<ms>;wf=<ms>;att=<n>;tls=<ms>;p_seq=...;p_aw=<ms>;p_rq=<ms>;p_dl=<ms>;p_dr=<ms>;p_rd=<0|1|2>;p_out=<token>`.
- Current-wake fields (known before the request is sent): `seq` (the NVS boot count, `app_main.c:313`), `bt` (time from app start to Wi-Fi start, `fp_wake_elapsed_ms()` at `state_machine.c:109`), `wf` (Wi-Fi connect plus time sync, already measured as `timing_out->wifi_ms`, `:109-113`), `att` (attempts; needs `s_retries` exposed, `wifi.c:27`), `tls` (`s_first_connect_ms`, `api_client.c:63`; only known after the connect, so it can only be the previous wake's value).
- Previous-wake fields, carried in a small `RTC_DATA_ATTR` struct written inside `enter_deep_sleep` (`app_main.c:128-145`, the single exit, which already calls `fp_panel_before_sleep`): `p_seq`, `p_aw` (`fp_wake_elapsed_ms()` at sleep entry), `p_rq`/`p_dl`/`p_dr` (from `s_timing`, `app_main.c:86`), `p_rd` (0 no draw, 1 drawn, 2 deferred), `p_out` (ok, or the fail step token). Failed wakes that never reach the server (Wi-Fi failure) are thereby reported by the next successful wake, with their step token, if RTC memory survived.
- A struct validity word (magic and a checksum) so a power-loss-cleared RTC area is dropped rather than reported (RTC is lost on brownout, as `app_main.c:18-21` says for the failure counter); absence is reported by omitting the `p_*` keys.
- Rejected: a second battery read at sleep entry to see the per-wake voltage drop. By my arithmetic one mAh is about 0.03 % of 3000 mAh, i.e. about 1 mV or less, below the ADC noise and the 1 mV quantisation; it would not measure per-wake energy.
- Rejected for this phase: any on-device current measurement (no hardware for it; deferred by CONTEXT).

### Schema and migration approach (recommended)
`ALTER TABLE device_health ADD COLUMN ...` for each nullable integer column, inside `init_schema`, guarded by `PRAGMA table_info(device_health)` so it is idempotent, preserving the `_SCHEMA_READY` once-per-process behaviour. Existing rows get NULL; frames that do not send the header produce NULL; nothing is rewritten. Risk to handle: the companion export opens `mode=ro` and cannot migrate, so if the companion process serves an export before the 30 s poll oneshot has run the migration after a deploy, the new SELECT would raise `sqlite3.OperationalError` (today mapped to a 503 at `export_api.py:147-148`). Make `export_device_health` fall back to the five-column SELECT on "no such column", and add a test for exactly that. The alternative (a separate `wake_stats` table, free per the docstring) avoids ALTER but needs a LEFT JOIN with a table-exists fallback in the same read-only path; either is acceptable, the planner should choose one in the first plan and record it. Both are additive and need no data migration. `UNIQUE(ts, battery_mv)` stays as the dedupe key.

## 3. The OTA and release path

### How a release is built, signed, published, applied
1. Build: `firmware/build.sh` in `espressif/idf:v5.3.1`; `SKYPANE_RELEASE_TAG=fw-vX.Y.Z` requires HEAD to carry exactly that tag on a clean tree, and the tag becomes the reported version (`build.sh:13-19`, `:91-113`).
2. CI trigger: pushing a `fw-v*` tag on a commit already on `main` runs `.github/workflows/firmware-release.yml`. The `guard` job checks tag shape and ancestry of `origin/main`; the `release` job runs in the `firmware-signing` GitHub environment, which needs a human reviewer's approval before the secret reaches the run (`firmware-release.yml:16-22`, `:28-93`). It rebuilds, runs the production-config and Log Line Contract checks, signs out of band with `espsecure.py sign_data --version 2` (RSA-3072, no network, container pinned by digest), verifies against the committed public key (`firmware/signing/skypane-signing-pubkey.pem`), checks the image descriptor, generates `release.json` and notes, and publishes a GitHub Release (`:95-203`).
3. Deploy: the workflow then dispatches `ci.yml` on `main`; the `deploy` job is behind the `production` environment reviewer gate and imports every release into `/opt/skypane/state/firmware/` (`deploy/README.md:437-478`). A release is then only "available".
4. Apply: nothing installs automatically. The operator chooses Install on the companion Update page, which schedules it; the frame sees the offer in a `/display` response and applies it at that wake (`deploy/README.md:476-479`; `state_machine.c:177-255`).
5. Frame-side gates: offer refused if battery at or below 3500 mV or unknown (`ota_policy.h:25`, `ota_policy.c:105-107`), below the version floor `fw-v1.0.0` (`Kconfig.projbuild:34-43`), same version, or a trial already pending; server-side attempt limit `MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3` (`firmware_registry.py:70`); signature check on update with the running app's embedded public key (`SIGNING.md`); hash and size as an early gate; a per-type monotonic build number at publish time (`deploy/README.md:481-490`).
6. Cost of one OTA to the battery: the offer triggers the UPDATING screen (a full panel redraw), the image download (stage budget 90 s, `wake_deadline.h:29`), a restart, a trial wake, and one more redraw to put the picture back because the UPDATING sentinel hash can never match (`state_machine.c:98`, `:201-203`). That is roughly three redraw-wake equivalents of charge at one moment, by my reading of the code; no committed figure. It also marks a discontinuity in any voltage trend (the new `fw_version` appears in the next row).

### Rollback
- Automatic: `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_APP_ROLLBACK_ENABLE=y` (`sdkconfig.defaults:22`); the new image boots on trial and is confirmed only after a successful poll, right before sleep (`app_main.c:387-395`); a crash or failed confirm returns to the previous image, and the next poll reports `rollback` or a `fail-*` token via `X-Ota-Result` (counted toward `MAX_ATTEMPTS`, `firmware_registry.py:97-101`).
- Voluntary: scheduling an older already-published release at or above the floor stays allowed ("Voluntary rollback ... stays allowed", `firmware/SIGNING.md`, Decision record; `firmware_registry.py:813-818`). So `fw-v1.0.2` can be re-offered from the Update page.
- Last resort: USB flash. An unsigned image does not boot (`SIGNING.md`), `firmware/flash.sh` verifies the signature before writing.

## 4. Where measurable awake time already lives, and an estimate

### What the server can and cannot infer today
- `device_health` holds one row per wake with the Caddy log time (seconds), battery, version, reason, RSSI. From it: the gap between successive rows. `gap - sleep_s` is the awake overhead PLUS the sleep-timer error, not awake time alone (shown below).
- The Caddy log itself (on the VPS only, not in the repo, and not exposed by the export route) also holds the image GET and firmware GET requests, which the tailer discards (`history_db.py:718`). From it one could infer retrospectively whether a wake downloaded (a redraw) and the download duration, if Caddy's `duration` and `request.tls.resumed` fields exist as I recall [ASSUMED; the Caddy docs page fetched does not list them and does not say whether `ts` is request start or completion]. It still cannot separate boot, Wi-Fi and draw time inside the gap, nor timer error.
- Therefore the device must report its own phases (WAKE-01). Only the sum is observable from outside.

### What the committed data show (computed this session; numbers are mine, reproducible)
Gap excess = (gap between consecutive rows) minus the configured `sleep_s`. Sources: `hardware/logs/battery-run2-export.jsonl` (60 s, fw-v1.0.2, 2026-10-05 to 10-12) and the 2026-11-03 export of the old-pack test, which lives on branch `claude/oldpack-two-cadence-2026-11-03` (not on the current branch; the "Two-cadence analysis, 2026-11-03" section of `hardware/FIELD-VALIDATION.md` and `hardware/oldpack_two_cadence.py` are also only there: the planner must treat merging that branch as a dependency). Rows with no telemetry were dropped; gaps longer than 150 s of excess (quiet hours) were excluded; all rows are fw-v1.0.2 and boot reason "rtc" [VERIFIED: tool].

| Cadence | Gaps used | Excess distribution | Reading |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| 60 s | 6192 | 1 to 2 s: 4145 (67 %); 36 to 38 s: 1950 (32 %, median 37 s) | Clean two modes: no-change vs redraw. |
| 900 s (2026-10-24 to 10-31 11:00Z) | 497 | median 34 s, quartiles 33 to 34, 10th/90th percentile 32/36; 464 of 497 (93 %) in 30 to 39 s; almost nothing below 10 s | Nearly every wake looks like a redraw wake, but 2.5 to 3 s shorter than the 60 s redraw mode. |
| 3600 s (2026-10-31 12:00Z to 11-03) | 54 | median 20 s, quartiles 18 to 21, range 12 to 27; 51 of 54 in 15 to 24 s | Matches neither mode (not about 1.5 s, not about 37 s). |

Hypothesis H-T (mine, arithmetic, NOT established): redraw wakes take a constant about 37.5 s awake, and the deep-sleep timer runs about 0.45 % short, so `excess = 37.5 - 0.0045 x sleep_s`: that gives 37.2 s at 60 s (observed 36 to 38), 33.3 s at 900 s (observed 34), 20.6 s at 3600 s (observed 20). It also makes the no-change mode 1.2 s at 60 s (observed 1 to 2). Under H-T every 3600 s wake is a redraw. Alternatives that the code cannot exclude: a different redraw duration at long intervals; Wi-Fi behaviour after long sleep. What decides it: device `p_aw` versus `gap - sleep_s`. Why a timer error is physically plausible (deep-sleep timing uses the internal RTC slow clock, calibrated against the crystal, with temperature drift) is training knowledge [ASSUMED]. Consequence if H-T holds: the CONTEXT statement that gaps are "a proxy of awake time" understates awake time by about 16 s at 3600 s.

Note also that H-T does not by itself explain the energy puzzle: if both cadences are redraw-dominated, the old-pack per-cycle charge is about 2.2 mAh at 900 s (8.6 mA x 934 s) and about 1.0 mAh at 3600 s (about 1 mA x 3620 s) by my arithmetic from the committed averages, which no constant wake cost plus a non-negative standing draw reproduces. A least-squares fit I ran on the three earlier cadences (rows: 328 s cycle, 10.1 mA, redraw share about 0.74 from the 27.8 s mean overhead; 74 s, 24 mA, 0.32; 934 s, 8.6 mA, 0.97) solved exactly gives standing 8.3 mA, no-change wake 0.44 mAh and redraw wake 0.065 mAh, which is non-physical (a redraw cheaper than a no-change wake); adding the 3600 s row (redraw share unknown, bounded 0 to 1) gives negative coefficients in every case. Inputs are rough (rated capacity, curve conversion, redraw shares inferred from gaps), so this is a demonstration that the existing aggregates cannot be fitted, not a result. The tool of WAKE-02 must therefore refuse rather than fit when the data cannot separate the unknowns, and must be run on per-wake device data.

### Main risks

### System data flow (telemetry)

   -> battery read -> fp_poll_once
        wifi connect (wf, att) --fail--> fail_and_sleep(step) --> RTC struct (p_out=fail:step)
        GET /device/v1/display  + X-Wake-Stats (current + previous-wake fields from RTC)

        download -> sha256 -> radio off -> panel draw          |  tailer: allowlist -> parse -> bounds
              |                                                v

```

```

### Pattern: single choke point for end-of-wake state
**What:** every wake ends in `enter_deep_sleep` (`app_main.c:128-145`); write the carry-forward there. **Why:** it already calls `fp_panel_before_sleep`, which writes the RTC panel guard values the same way (`panel.c:62-66`), so no path can skip it.

### Pattern: RTC struct with validity word
Follow `tls_session.c:62-64` (`RTC_DATA_ATTR`), add a magic and checksum; drop on mismatch.

### Anti-patterns to avoid
- Reading the URI for data: the tailer requires the exact URI (`history_db.py:718`).
- A second request per wake to carry telemetry.
- Adding a header without adding it to `clear_request_headers` (leaks it onto the image request; the existing comment at `api_client.c:67-71` is the reason that list exists).
- Presenting a voltage-derived figure as a current measurement.
- Using `gap - sleep_s` as awake time.

## Don't Hand-Roll

| Problem | Don't build | Use instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Header validation | ad-hoc string splitting | a strict regex parser modelled on `parse_battery_mv` (`byos_server.py:776-796`) and `parse_fw_version_header` | Header is untrusted; bounds and fail-to-NULL. |
| Schema change | a migration framework | `PRAGMA table_info` guarded `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN` in `init_schema`, or a new table | The repo has no migration mechanism and does not need one for additive nullable columns. |
| Reproducible analysis output | a notebook | a stdlib tool with a committed JSON regenerated by a test | The pattern of `hardware/logtools.py` and `test_logtools_model.py`. |

| Release signing | local signing as the normal path | `firmware-release.yml` | The key stays in the `firmware-signing` environment. |

**Key insight:** this phase is about measuring honestly; every shortcut that derives time from request gaps or energy from a constant per wake repeats the mistake the existing aggregates already show.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: gap minus sleep is not awake time
**What goes wrong:** "20 s over sleep at 3600 s" read as a 20 s wake. **Why:** sleep-timer error subtracts or adds time proportional to `sleep_s` (H-T). **Avoid:** use device `p_aw`. **Warning sign:** the excess shrinks as the interval grows.

### Pitfall 2: the instrumentation changes wake cost
**What goes wrong:** an extra flash write or request becomes part of the baseline. **Avoid:** RTC only; one header; review the diff for cost neutrality.

### Pitfall 3: new column breaks the read-only export
**What goes wrong:** 503 until the poll oneshot migrates. **Avoid:** fallback SELECT and a test for an unmigrated database.

### Pitfall 4: freeze list and test disagree in spirit
**What goes wrong:** a history-only parse passes `test_phase47_freeze.py` (it only measures `battery_state`, `device_report` and stdout) while contradicting the file. **Avoid:** owner-agreed amendment.

### Pitfall 5: RTC carry-forward is wrong after power loss or a skipped wake
**Avoid:** validity word, `p_seq` equal to `seq - 1` check on the server, omit rather than guess.

### Pitfall 6: fixed cadence assumptions in analysis
The effective period is `sleep_s` plus awake time and timer error; use observed gaps and on-device `p_aw`, never the nominal interval (the existing run-two reference-interval discussion in `hardware/BATTERY-RUN.md:699-706` makes the same point).

### Pitfall 7: touching the panel sequence
Any change in `epd13in3e.c` risks a corrupted colour refresh or a stuck rail; keep `epd_sleep` always called (`panel.c:106`) and the rail off.
