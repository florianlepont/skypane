# Phase 47.1: Wake-energy instrumentation and awake-time reduction - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-11-03
**Status:** Ready for research and planning
**Source:** The owner conversation of 2026-10-12 to 2026-11-03 and the recorded evidence (no separate discuss-phase session; each locked item below was said or accepted by the owner, or is a fact in the repository)

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

The old-pack battery tests have shown where the problem is, and it is not the sleep current. Evidence (all on the old 3000 mAh pack, rated, never measured; the voltage-to-charge map is a rated curve):
- Run one, 300 s, about 10 mA, 11 days. Run two, 60 s, about 24 mA, 5 days (`hardware/BATTERY-MODEL.md`, `hardware/logs/`).
- Old-pack test, `hardware/FIELD-VALIDATION.md` section "Two-cadence analysis, 2026-11-03": about 6 to 9 mA at 900 s and about 1 mA at 3600 s. The standing draw is at most about 1 mA. The cost of a wake is not constant (about 0.5 to 2 mAh), and the average current falls by 6 to 9 times when the wake count falls by 4, which a constant wake cost cannot explain.
- Gaps between wakes (a proxy of awake time) were about 34 s over the configured sleep at 900 s, about 20 s at 3600 s, about 14 s in run two, about 28 s in run one.
- Unverified hypothesis of the owner and Claude: the largest consumer is the e-paper refresh when the image changes, plus Wi-Fi connection time; both vary with traffic and conditions, not with the interval.

What is missing is the cost of each wake and of each phase of a wake. This phase adds that measurement and then uses it to reduce the awake time.

Requirements: WAKE-01 (per-wake telemetry stored and exported), WAKE-02 (a tested per-wake energy model with its uncertainty), WAKE-03 (measured reduction of the awake time with no behavioural change), WAKE-04 (the model checked on the replacement pack).

Out of scope: a new discharge study as such, solar or wall power, a different battery chemistry, changes to the companion UI beyond what shows the new telemetry in the existing Health data or export, anything that changes what the frame displays.
</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Owner facts and choices (locked)
- The owner is a French speaker and not an engineer; anything the owner has to do is written in plain French, step by step. Repository documents stay in English.
- The owner refused the multimeter-in-series measurement. It stays optional and is never a dependency of this phase.
- Autonomy wins over freshness when they conflict (earlier decision), and six months between charges is the owner's goal with the replacement pack. The honest finding so far: plausible at hourly wakes, not at 900 s or shorter.
- The firmware can be updated over the air (the OTA phase is done); firmware releases are built in the `espressif/idf:v5.3.1` container (`firmware/build.sh`) and signed (`firmware/SIGNING.md`). Any release is a field change to a battery frame: nothing is flashed or pushed to the frame on the owner's behalf; the owner approves and applies it. Never approve or run a production deploy on the owner's behalf.
- The 3600 s run on the old pack (2026-10-31 to the bilan of 2026-11-07) and the replacement pack (Soldered 333290, 10000 mAh, kept aside, not yet checked or connected) belong to Phase 47. A firmware change alters wake costs, so no firmware release lands during an open measurement window without the owner's agreement; instrumentation first, awake-time cuts only after the baseline is recorded.
- The companion has a read-only, bearer-token export of the `device_health` history (`GET /api/export/battery-history`, deployed and verified); Claude fetches it through the platform's network-secret proxy and never sees the token.

### Evidence rules (carried from Phases 45 to 47)
- Figures come from committed, tested tools or are labelled arithmetic or judgement by Claude. Ratings (3000 mAh, 10000 mAh) and the discharge curve are not measurements; the top of the curve (above 4000 mV) is not calibrated and the tools refuse a band there.
- Owner-reported facts are labelled with their date.
- A cost per wake is an energy estimate derived from voltage and timing, not a current measurement; the documents say so every time.

### Claude's Discretion
- The telemetry transport (extra request headers like the existing `X-Battery-Mv`, or another existing channel), the field set and their units, the storage layout in the history database, and the export format extension, provided the device protocol stays backward compatible with frames that do not send the new fields.
- How many plans, their order and waves; the file layout of the model tool and its evidence; which awake-time reductions are worth doing and in which order, chosen from the measured breakdown, not from a prior.
- Whether the instrumentation firmware and the reduction firmware ship as one release or two.
</decisions>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- Telemetry per wake: boot reason and reset count (existing), time from boot to Wi-Fi associated and to IP, number of Wi-Fi attempts, RSSI, time of the poll request and the download, whether the panel was redrawn and for how long, total awake time from wake to sleep entry, firmware version (existing).
- Model: for each wake, awake time by phase; the energy per phase from a small number of unknown currents fitted against voltage-trend segments from several cadences and traffic conditions; the tool reports the fit, the residual and the range, and refuses to fit when the data cannot separate the unknowns.
- Awake-time cuts to evaluate from the breakdown: fast Wi-Fi reconnection (cached channel and access point, reuse of the address), a lighter secure-connection setup, smaller responses, skipping the download when the hash is unchanged (already done), lower CPU frequency or radio power where safe, redraw only when the change matters; and, at policy level, adaptive intervals and a button-driven refresh (noted for the owner, not necessarily in this phase).
</specifics>

<deferred>
## Deferred Ideas

- Adaptive wake intervals chosen by the server from traffic, and a button-driven refresh with a long default interval, as product features.
- A hardware current measurement (an inline logger or the optional multimeter procedure) to replace the voltage-based energy estimate.
- A third controlled discharge study (BAT-05) if the model still leaves a decision-blocking uncertainty.
</deferred>
