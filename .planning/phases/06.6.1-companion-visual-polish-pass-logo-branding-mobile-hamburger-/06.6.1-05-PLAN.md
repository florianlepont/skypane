<objective>
Replace the horizontally-scrollable nav strip with a real hamburger menu below 960px: a toggle button in the header that expands a panel underneath it, holding the five nav links and the relocated theme picker, closing on a second tap or on Escape, with `aria-expanded` kept honest throughout.

Purpose: real-device testing during 06.5-03 found the strip effectively hid Health, Airlines, History and Preview behind a swipe with no affordance — even after the flexbox crush bug (D-00, commit `b90ed88`) was fixed, only three or four tabs were reachable on a real phone. This phase deletes the pattern rather than tuning it again.
Output: the project's second JavaScript file, a pre-auth route to serve it, a new nav renderer, a restructured header, and eight checks — six new, two retargeted.
</objective>

## What this plan removes, and why removal is correct

`companion/layout.py`'s `_nav_html()` and the `<nav class="nav-bar">` it renders are **deleted**, not hidden. The existing 960px block already sets `.site-header { display: none }` at desktop width, and plan 06.6.1-01 removed the strip's own CSS rules; a renderer whose output is unstyled at every width and invisible above 960px is dead code, and `06.6.1-UI-SPEC.md`'s Layout Contract says so explicitly ("remove it rather than leaving an orphaned, unreachable CSS rule").

After this plan there are exactly **two** live navigation renderers — `sidebar_nav()` for ≥960px and `_mobile_nav_html()` for <960px — both fed by the same `_nav_links()` helper. The UI-SPEC's Component Patterns row calls this "three renderers"; that phrasing was written before the strip's removal was settled, and plan 06.6.1-01's Reconciliation 2 records the resolution. The invariant that actually matters is unchanged and must stay true: **one shared link-building helper, never a third independent iteration of `NAV_TABS`.**
