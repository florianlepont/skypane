<objective>
Have a human actually look at what the previous five plans built — on a real phone and on a real desktop browser — and either sign it off or report what is wrong.

Purpose, stated plainly because it is the whole reason this phase exists: `06.6.1-CONTEXT.md` records that 06.3, 06.4 and 06.5 were all "visually verified" through `read_page`/`get_page_text`/computed-style inspection, with **no human ever looking at a rendered page**. When the developer finally opened the app on their own phone during 06.5-03, they immediately found a real CSS flexbox bug (D-00) and a batch of design defects the automated sign-off had passed clean. D-05 locks this phase to the same formal rigour 06.3 used **plus** the step 06.3 skipped.
Output: a recorded verdict with verbatim findings.
</objective>

## This checkpoint may not be satisfied by inspection

Computed-style reads, DOM assertions, `getComputedStyle` colour values and markup greps are **not** a substitute for any item below, and must not be offered as one. Every one of the five preceding plans already carries automated gates for the things automation can check; this plan exists exclusively for what it cannot:

- whether a system-serif stack actually resolved to a serif face on the developer's own devices, or silently fell back to sans;
- whether two colours 16° apart in hue are *actually* distinguishable to a human at a glance;
- whether a 44px tap target is reachable with a real thumb on a real phone held one-handed;
- whether a shadow reads as relief or as smudge on a real panel at real brightness;
- whether 7 columns actually fit, at the developer's actual laptop width, with the Timestamp column's actual current content;
- whether the whole thing looks good.

If a real phone is genuinely unavailable in the session, the correct action is to **pause this checkpoint and report that**, not to downgrade it to inspection.

  <what-built>
Five plans of visual and structural change to the companion web app:

- **Plan 01** — the whole design-token layer: a warm serif family for headings and the wordmark, a terracotta accent replacing the blue, explicit accent-hover, card shadows and softer radii, warm off-white light-mode surfaces, airier global spacing, and the removal of the horizontally-scrollable nav strip's CSS.
- **Plan 02** — History's table cut from 9 columns to 7, merging Callsign·Hex and Type·Airline into single one-line cells.
- **Plan 03** — the Health anomaly banner's redundant detail list removed and its copy re-pointed; the battery-trend chart moved out of the dashboard grid into its own full-width card section.
- **Plan 04** — a five-symbol inline icon sprite, per-tile icons tinted by status, and a notification dot on the Health nav link driven by a live anomaly signal.
- **Plan 05** — the hamburger menu: a toggle in the header opening a push-down dropdown holding the five nav links and the relocated theme picker, closing on a second tap or on Escape.
  </what-built>

  <how-to-verify>
Work through these groups in order. For each item answer **pass**, **fail** or **marginal**, and say what you saw. "Marginal" is a real and useful answer — record it rather than rounding it to pass.

**A. On a real phone (this group is the point of the whole checkpoint — do not substitute a desktop browser's narrow window)**

1. Open the app. Is the hamburger button obvious as a menu control without being told what it is?
2. Tap it. Does the panel expand *below* the header and push the page content down, rather than covering it or taking over the screen?
3. Are all five destinations — Config, Health, Airlines, History, Preview — visible in the open panel at once, with no scrolling or swiping?
4. Is the current page's link visually marked as current?
5. Is the theme picker inside the open panel, and does tapping Light / Dark / Auto actually change the theme?
6. Tap the hamburger again. Does the panel close?
7. Are the links and the theme buttons comfortable to hit with a thumb, held one-handed — not merely reachable on a careful second attempt?
8. Navigate to two different tabs through the menu. Does anything jump, flash open on load, or leave the menu stuck open?
9. Scroll the Health page. Does the battery chart use the full width, and does tapping a point still reveal its exact reading and timestamp? (This one is a **regression** check on Phase 06.5's shipped behaviour, not a new feature.)
10. Open History. It still scrolls horizontally on a phone by design — but is the merged Callsign·Hex / Type·Airline layout readable, and are the rows still the same height as before, i.e. one line per cell?

**B. Colour and typography, on the phone and on the desktop, in both light and dark**

11. Do the page headings and the "SkyPane" wordmark render in an actual **serif** face? (If the serif stack failed to resolve you will see the same sans-serif as the body text — this is the specific silent-failure mode to look for.)
12. Do body text, table cells, form labels and the nav links stay **sans-serif**? Anything dense or tabular rendering in serif is a defect.
13. Is the new accent orange clearly a *different colour* from the amber "warning" status dot, at a glance, without comparing them side by side? Find a page showing both — the Health page with a warn-state tile is the reliable one.
14. Switch to dark mode. Does the accent still read as the accent, and do the shadows still read as relief rather than as a dark smudge?
15. In light mode, does the background read as a warm off-white, or does it read as dirty / yellowed?

**C. Card relief and spacing, on the desktop**

16. Do the Health and Airlines cards read as raised cards with a soft shadow, rather than as flat outlined boxes?
17. Does a card visibly lift when you hover it?
18. Does the page feel more open than before — more breathing room between blocks and around the content column — without feeling empty or scattered?
19. Do the four Health icons read clearly at their small size, and does each one's colour match its own card's status?

**D. Health page structure**

20. Trigger the anomaly dataset (the restart command from Task 1). Does a dot appear beside "Health" in the sidebar navigation, and again in the phone's dropdown menu?
21. With the anomaly active, does the banner say what needs attention without repeating itself, and is there no bulleted list under it?
22. Switch back to the healthy dataset. Does the dot disappear entirely, with nothing left in its place?
23. Is the Battery trend section clearly its own full-width block below the three Overview cards, rather than a fourth card?

**E. History density, on your actual laptop**

24. Open History at your normal browser window size on the 13" laptop. Do all 7 columns fit without horizontal scrolling? If not, say **which column** is pushing it over — the Timestamp column is the known suspect, because Phase 06.6's shorter relative-time format has not shipped yet, and that is an accepted interim state rather than a failure of this phase.

**F. Nothing else broke**

25. Visit Config, Airlines and Preview. Does each look coherent under the new tokens, with nothing overlapping, clipped or unstyled?
26. Resize the desktop window slowly from wide down to phone width. Does the sidebar hand over to the hamburger cleanly at the changeover, with no width where both are visible and no width where neither is?
27. Anything else that just looks wrong to you, whether or not this phase caused it.
  </how-to-verify>
