<objective>
Answer, empirically, the question no document can answer: can a public ADS-B aggregator actually see aircraft at the low-altitude and on-ground segment of Orly's runway 3, or does the plane-detection approach need a local RTL-SDR receiver?

Per D-01 this is tested API-first, because it costs nothing, needs no hardware, needs no antenna, and carries no legal ambiguity. Per D-02 the local RTL-SDR fallback is reached for only if the aggregator path proves insufficient. Per D-03 this reverses PROJECT.md's current framing, and PROJECT.md and REQUIREMENTS.md are updated only after this plan produces a result - not before.

RESEARCH.md's Pitfall 3 is the trap this plan is shaped to avoid: general "Paris has dense feeder coverage" claims describe cruise-altitude overflights, which any nearby feeder sees trivially. Near-ground reception at a specific runway depends on whether some contributing feeder has line of sight to that runway segment, and no coverage map guarantees it. The only evidence that counts is aircraft observed inside the geofence at low altitude during real traffic.

This track is deliberately unwired from the device firmware. It validates groundwork for PLANE-03; PLANE-03 itself is delivered in Phase 2. Nothing here is integrated into the poll loop this phase.

Purpose: Phase 2 builds the entire plane view on top of whichever data source wins here. Getting the wrong answer cheaply now is the difference between a Phase 2 that ships and a Phase 2 that discovers mid-build that its data source cannot see the runway.
Output: A reusable geofenced query tool, a recorded sampling window over real traffic, and a written go or no-go on the RTL-SDR fallback.
</objective>

    Write `adsb-test/sample_window.py`, a stdlib-only unattended sampler. Flags: `--minutes` for the window length defaulting to 90; `--interval` for seconds between samples of each provider, defaulting to 30, which stays far inside the one-per-second limit; `--out` for the output directory, defaulting to a samples directory under adsb-test; and `--geofence` defaulting to runway3.json.

    Each sample writes one JSON Lines record per provider containing an ISO-8601 UTC timestamp, the provider name, the total aircraft count returned, and the full list of in-bbox aircraft with hex, callsign, altitude (numeric or the on-ground sentinel), ground speed, latitude, longitude and, when present, the vertical rate.     Write `adsb-test/analyze_samples.py`, a stdlib-only analyser over a run's JSONL files, printing a markdown-formatted report and exiting 0. Per provider it computes: the number of samples taken and how many returned an error; the count of distinct ICAO hex codes ever seen inside the bbox; the count of distinct hex codes ever seen inside the bbox at or below the altitude ceiling; the count of distinct hex codes ever seen with the on-ground sentinel; for the aircraft with the most samples, the median and maximum gap in seconds between consecutive position updates; and the lowest numeric altitude observed inside the bbox. It also computes the overlap - how many hex codes both providers saw, and how many either provider saw uniquely - since a provider seeing aircraft the other misses is directly decision-relevant.

    Define the viability threshold explicitly in the analyser output so the decision is not a matter of impression. A provider passes when, over the sampled window, it observed at least 6 distinct aircraft inside the bbox at or below the 3000-foot ceiling, at least 1 aircraft carrying the on-ground sentinel, and a median position-update gap of 15 seconds or less for its best-tracked aircraft. The analyser prints PASS or FAIL per provider against each of those three conditions, and an overall per-provider verdict.
