<expectation_note id="N-12-06-01">
**A forced render stays on the glass until the next real detection.** The poll loop holds
the last panel through detection gaps by design (the behaviour plan 08-06 documented).
Every forcing step below ends the same way: restart the poll timer. If the session ends
with that timer stopped, the frame sits on a synthetic test image indefinitely and looks
like it is working. The timer's final state and a real post-restart poll cycle are both
acceptance criteria.

**This phase can leave the frame dark.** Unlike every prior on-glass session, this one
toggles a setting that suppresses rendering entirely. Ending the session with
`display_enabled` false would leave a frame that looks broken and whose next real update is
five minutes away at best. Confirming the toggle is back ON, in the saved config and on the
glass, is an acceptance criterion.

**Each observation costs a full panel refresh**, roughly 31.5 seconds on this hardware
(plan 08-06's measurement). Batch the forced renders in the order given rather than
tweaking and re-looking one change at a time.

**Confirm the deployed paths before forcing anything.** This plan writes the expected
values (application root `/opt/skypane`, service user `skypane`, units
`skypane-poll.timer`/`skypane-poll.service`) from plans 08-06 and 09-04's confirmed record
— the operator should re-confirm them against the deploy directory and environment file on
the host rather than trusting this document blindly.

**Read `12-02-SUMMARY.md` before Step B.** It records the observed wrap line count for the
body string, measured against the real `safe_width` rather than assumed. `12-UI-SPEC.md`
anticipated two lines; check what actually shipped and ask about what is actually there.
</expectation_note>

This session may apply bounded corrections directly, matching plans 08-06 and 09-04's
precedent. `12-UI-SPEC.md` defines the escalation ladder for this phase's one flagged risk,
and it is reproduced verbatim below rather than paraphrased:

> If the check finds the three hold screens too similar to distinguish at a glance, the
> acceptable follow-up adjustments — in order of preference, least to most invasive,
> mirroring 10-UI-SPEC.md's own escalation ladder — are: (1) nothing, if the differing
> heading text alone proves sufficient in practice; (2) a slightly bolder/larger heading
> treatment for this screen than the other two, still within White/Black; (3) only as a
> last resort, a distinct flat fill color for this screen alone (still 6-palette-legal,
> still passing `_assert_legal_palette()`). Do not skip the real-preview check and ship
> option (2) or (3) speculatively — build option (1) first and test against it.

Also bounded: a body-text point-size reduction if the glass shows the wrapped lines
crowding the safe-box margins, and a wording tightening of the body string if it proves
unreadable at distance — provided the replacement still promises no return time and still
avoids fault vocabulary (D-04).

What is **not** bounded, and requires an explicit, recorded developer instruction before it
is touched:

- Changing the off-state cadence away from D-01's 300 seconds, in either direction. The
  battery trade-off behind that number rests on an explicitly un-measured assumption whose
  resolution 12-CONTEXT.md defers to `DEVICE-05`; re-opening it from a visual session would
  be optimising against the same unmeasured assumption from the other side.
- Reversing either axis of D-05, or collapsing the two axes into one rule.
- Making a hold-to-hold move repaint (D-07), however tempting it looks on the glass.
- Reverting to a blank field or a blank field with a corner mark — both were considered and
  rejected in discussion (D-03), and a blank field is precisely what erodes the CFG-05
  source-fault icon's meaning.
- Any firmware change. D-01 requires none, and 12-CONTEXT.md records that the willingness
  to make one was noted only to widen the option space for a deferred refinement.

Every correction applied in session must be re-rendered and re-confirmed on the glass
before its step is considered closed, and `scripts/run-all-tests.sh` must pass again before
the session ends.

  <what-built>
    A remote on/off toggle for the frame, and a new panel screen for the off state — the
    manual, immediate sibling of Phase 10's scheduled quiet hours:

    - A single **Display** checkbox, last on the companion Settings page, saving through
      the same form as every other setting. Unchecking it turns the frame dark.
    - A new **DISPLAY OFF** panel screen: flat White, Black ink, a centred all-caps heading
      and a wrapped body line saying the panel was switched off from the companion page and
      can be turned back on there. It deliberately makes **no** return-time promise — a
      manual toggle has no scheduled end, unlike the quiet-hours screen's "Back at HH:MM".
    - While off, the device checks in every **300 seconds** instead of using its configured
      wake interval, and the server produces no new flight panel. That is why switching back
      on takes up to about five minutes.
    - When the toggle and quiet hours are both active, the toggle decides **what the panel
      shows** but the **longest sleep wins** — so the device never wakes more often with the
      display off than quiet hours alone would have made it.
    - Moving between the two hold states never repaints. A quiet window ending while the
      toggle is still off leaves the panel exactly as it was.

    Every judgment behind the new screen's composition — the heading wording, the body
    wording, the font sizes, the flat White field — was made from screen-preview PNGs and
    from the deliberate choice to mirror the quiet-hours screen's shape. **None of it has
    been near the real panel, and this phase now puts three visually similar full-bleed
    White/Black centred-text screens into rotation** (empty, quiet hours, display off) that
    have never been compared side by side on real ink.
  </what-built>

  <how-to-verify>
    **Setup.**
    1. `SSH_TARGET=<your ssh target> ./deploy/deploy.sh`
    2. Confirm live detection: `ssh <target> 'sudo journalctl -u skypane-poll -n 30 --no-pager'` shows recent cycles.
    3. Stop the timer: `ssh <target> 'sudo systemctl stop skypane-poll.timer'`
    4. Confirm the state path before writing to it (read it from the environment file or unit on the host rather than assuming).

    Each forced panel: `ssh <target> 'sudo -u skypane /opt/skypane/venv/bin/python3 /opt/skypane/server/plane/render.py <flags> --out /opt/skypane/state/panel.bin'`, then power-cycle or wait for the device's wake. ~31.5s per refresh, expected.

    **Step A — the three hold screens, back to back, in this order.** Force each and leave
    it up long enough to look at properly before moving on:
    - `--state display_off`
    - `--state quiet_hours --quiet-hours-until 07:00`
    - `--state empty`

    Then force `--state display_off` once more, so the last thing seen is the new screen
    with both siblings fresh in memory. These are `12-UI-SPEC.md`'s three locked questions,
    asked verbatim:

    1. **`DISPLAY OFF` reads as clearly distinct from `QUIET HOURS` and from the empty
       state's own heading** at a normal glance-from-across-the-room viewing distance — not
       just distinguishable on close inspection.
    2. **The wrapped two-line body text** (`Switched off from the companion page. Turn it
       back on there anytime.`) renders legibly and doesn't crowd the safe-box margins at
       the 40px body size — this body string is longer than the quiet-hours screen's own
       `"Back at HH:MM"` and has not been visually confirmed at this size before.
    3. **The screen does not read as an error/fault state** to a first-time viewer glancing
       at the frame without context — the specific ambiguity D-03 was written to avoid.

    For question 1, ask explicitly from across the room, not at arm's length — the failure
    mode is three screens that are all obviously different up close and indistinguishable
    at glance distance. For question 3, if anyone else is around, ask them what they think
    the frame is doing before saying anything about it.

    **Step B — the body text specifically.** Standing at the panel, check the wrapped
    line count matches what `12-02-SUMMARY.md` recorded, and that the block sits centred
    with comfortable margins rather than pressing on the safe box. Ask: does the sentence
    read as a calm statement of fact, or does anything about it read as an apology or a
    warning?

    **Step C — the device-health indicators on the new screen.** Force
    `--state display_off --battery-low` and, separately, the source-fault variant, using
    whichever flags `build_parser()` exposes for them. Ask: do both indicators sit clear of
    the centred text block and read the same way they do on the empty and quiet-hours
    screens?

    **Step D — the real operator loop, with the timer running. This is the step that
    proves the phase.** Restart the poll timer first: `ssh <target> 'sudo systemctl start
    skypane-poll.timer'`.
    1. On the companion Settings page, uncheck **Enable display** and save. Note the wall
       time.
    2. Watch the frame. It should repaint to DISPLAY OFF once, then hold. Record how long
       it took.
    3. Leave it off for at least three consecutive device check-ins (roughly 15 minutes) and
       confirm the panel does **not** repaint again in that time — the hold is silent.
    4. Re-check **Enable display** and save. Note the wall time.
    5. Confirm the live flight board comes back, and record how long it took. Up to about
       five minutes is the expected and accepted behaviour (D-02), not a defect — but a
       return that takes materially longer than five minutes is a real finding worth
       recording.
    6. Ask: as an operator, does "off" behave the way you wanted when you asked for this?
       Is the roughly-five-minute return acceptable in practice, or does it feel wrong in a
       way the discussion did not anticipate?

    **Step E — the overlap, if quiet hours can be reached conveniently.** Optional but
    valuable, and the only real-hardware look at D-05/D-07. With the display toggle off,
    temporarily set a quiet-hours window that starts within a few minutes, and confirm the
    panel does **not** repaint when the window opens — it should still show DISPLAY OFF.
    Then let the window end (or shorten it) with the toggle still off and confirm it does
    **not** repaint then either. Restore the original quiet-hours setting afterward and say
    plainly in the report whether this step was run or skipped.

    **Step F — teardown. Required.**
    1. Confirm **Enable display** is checked and saved.
    2. `ssh <target> 'sudo systemctl is-active skypane-poll.timer'` returns `active`.
    3. `ssh <target> 'sudo journalctl -u skypane-poll -n 20 --no-pager'` shows at least one real cycle after the restart.
    4. The frame is showing the live board, not a forced test image and not the off screen.
  </how-to-verify>
