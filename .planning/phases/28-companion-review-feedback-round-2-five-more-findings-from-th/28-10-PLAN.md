<objective>
28-08 restored the save bar's mechanism and retargeted the three fast JS-contract checks.
This plan does the slow half: `companion/test_browser_ux.py`.

The suite currently proves the auto-save model. Seventeen of its checks reach through
`_wait_for_saved()` / `_wait_for_saving()` / `_save_status_text()` into a
`[data-save-status]` region that 28-08 deleted. Those helpers and those call sites have to
become bar-shaped.

**The trap this plan exists to avoid is calling the wrong ones mechanical.** Most of the
seventeen only ever used `_wait_for_saved()` as a synonym for "the edit has committed" —
for those, swapping the wait for a bar wait is genuinely a no-op on the check's meaning.
But **seven** of them assert things the restoration *inverts* or *deletes*: a guard that
disarms on commit (it now stays armed), a text sequence that no longer exists, a bar that
must never be visible (it now must be), a fetch body that is now a native POST, a toast the
settings form no longer raises, a status region that was proven NOT fixed (the bar is). A
mechanical swap on any of those produces a check that compiles, passes, and proves nothing —
or worse, proves the retired contract.

So the triage below is explicit, per check, by name. Nine mechanical. Seven rewritten by
hand with per-check instructions. One removed.

**The standing rule for the whole plan:** every one of the seventeen either keeps an
equivalent-or-stronger assertion after retargeting, or has its removal explicitly named in
the SUMMARY with a reason. Silent assertion-weakening is the failure mode; it is not
detectable by a green suite, which is exactly why it is written down as a criterion here.

Purpose: move the browser suite from proving the auto-save to proving the bar, without
losing coverage on the way.
Output: bar-shaped helpers, seventeen retargeted owners, one named removal.
</objective>
