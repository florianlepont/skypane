<measurement>
Reproduced before changing anything, with a real Chromium tab against a real
`companion/app.py` subprocess, disclosure forced open, at 320/360/390/768/1280 x two languages.

Baseline — every number in the brief confirmed:

  lang  wrap cw  table    col0 (timestamp)  col1 (mV)  doc.scrollWidth
  FR    308      431.5    301.7             129.8      390  (closed AND open)
  EN    308      369.0    243.9             125.1      390  (closed AND open)

`overflow-x: auto` on the wrap, `min-width: max-content` on the table, `white-space: normal`
on the cell. 302 + 130 = 432 against 308 -> 124px of overflow, and the PAGE never moves.
That is the whole reason this survived: the wrapper scrolls, not the document.

Three candidate treatments were then measured, not argued:

  treatment                          320/FR   360/FR   390/FR   768/FR   row height
  (C1) stack, keep max-content       +59      +19      0        0        70.5 at EVERY width
  (C2) release max-content           0        0        0        0        75 narrow / 51 wide
  (C3) C2 + nowrap atoms             +26      0        0        0        75 narrow / 51 wide

C1 is the registry's treatment (22-12). It is REJECTED on its own numbers: stacking lowers
the max-content floor from 432 to 297, which clears 390px by exactly 0px and still overflows
at 360px and 320px. It also forces two lines at 1280px, where there is 830px of room.

C2 is chosen. At 360px and 390px the natural break lands on the exact semantic boundary in
both languages — "31 juil. 08:00" / "(il y a 44 j)", "31 Jul 08:00" / "(44d ago)" — because
the only break opportunity near the middle is the space before "(", and French already
carries a non-breaking space inside "44 j". At 768px and 1280px it stays on ONE line.

C3 (adding nowrap atoms via new spans) buys atom integrity at 320px but STILL overflows
there (+26 FR), so it pays a markup change across 86 call-site references for a width it
does not actually fix. Not taken.

Why releasing the floor is right here and was right to reject for the registry:
the floor exists to stop SHORT OPAQUE CODES cropping. The registry has two such columns
(prefix, example callsign — "EXEMPLE D'INDIC"), which have no safe internal break point.
This table has exactly two columns: a 4-digit integer (one token, zero break opportunities,
so the floor is inert for it) and a timestamp whose every break opportunity is a real word
boundary. The floor protects nothing here that is not already safe, while forbidding the
one break that makes the table fit.
</measurement>
