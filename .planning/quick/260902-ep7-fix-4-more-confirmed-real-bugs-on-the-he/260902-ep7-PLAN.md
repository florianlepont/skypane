--- Step 2: the direction taken, and why it is stronger than the brief's second suggestion ---

The brief's second direction — move the axis-label TEXT out of SVG-scaled coordinate space — identifies the real root tension correctly: geometry can scale, text legibility and the D-02 tap-target floor cannot. Take it, and then take it one step further, because once the text is out there is no longer any reason for a scaling transform to exist at all.

An inline `<svg>` with NO `viewBox` establishes no `viewBox`-to-viewport transform: one user unit IS one CSS pixel, and percentage-valued geometry attributes resolve against the element's own rendered viewport. So:
  - `r="8"` on a hit target is exactly 16 CSS px of diameter, at every container width, forever. `r="3"` markers, `stroke-width="2"` and a 10px label are likewise exact.
  - `cx="47.3%"` places a point at 47.3% of whatever width the card actually gives the element, with no server-side knowledge of that width.

  - The chart's plot spans 0% to 100% of its own box at every width — "fills the card" becomes a property of the layout rather than a value someone tuned.

Verify the SVG facts yourself before relying on them: percentages are permitted on `<line>`'s `x1`/`y1`/`x2`/`y2`, on `<circle>`'s `cx`/`cy`, and on `<rect>`'s `x`/`y`/`width`/`height`; each attribute resolves independently, so a `<rect>` may mix a percentage `y` with an absolute `height`.
