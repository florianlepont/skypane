<objective>
Three places in this repository state, in the codebase's own voice and as settled fact, that the panel vendor recommends updating the display once per day and that their published reliability work ran at a specific refresh interval, below which the panel is off a validated envelope. Two of those places (`firmware/main/Kconfig.projbuild`'s `FP_MIN_REFRESH_SPACING_S` help text and `firmware/main/panel_guard.h`'s module docstring) present it as the reason the 60 s refresh floor exists at all. The third (`server/poll_loop.py`) cites the firmware help text as a supporting coincidence for an unrelated server-side constant.

That claim could not be substantiated. The real component was identified first: the physical panel behind Seeed SKU E-6569 — the 13.3" Spectra 6 in the XIAO ePaper DIY Kit EE02 this project runs — is the Good Display GDEP133C02, matched on resolution (1200x1600), active area (202.8 x 270.4 mm), weight and connector spec. Its full 29-page official datasheet was fetched and read, and Seeed's product page, wiki and forum were searched. Neither the interval figure, nor the once-per-day recommendation, nor any cycle-count-based wear or lifespan rating appears in any of them. The datasheet's own "Reliability Test" section covers environmental stress only — temperature and humidity under storage and operation, thermal shock, ESD — and says nothing about refresh-cycle endurance.

What the datasheet does document about refresh frequency points the other way. Section 13 ("Precautions"), item 5, warns that a panel *not* refreshed every 24 hours may exhibit ghosting or image sticking, and recommends refreshing at least that often. That is a minimum-frequency recommendation guarding against a static image burning in, not a maximum-rate limit guarding against wear — close to the opposite of what this codebase says it is. For this device the recommendation is close to inert anyway: a departure board watching real airport traffic will not sit unchanged for 24 hours in normal operation.

So the numbers stay and the reasoning changes. 60 s and 90 s are defensible on their own terms — a full redraw takes a measured ~31.5 s of visibly churning glass and spends battery on a device whose entire architecture exists to minimise draw — and that is what the comments should say. What they must stop doing is borrowing authority from a vendor document that does not contain the claim. A false citation is worse than no citation: it forecloses the question for the next reader, who reasonably assumes someone already checked.

The correction must not overshoot either. "No cycle-count rating was found in the document consulted" is not "the panel never wears out". The replacement text says the first and explicitly declines to say the second.

Purpose: stop asserting an unverifiable vendor claim as the justification for a real engineering constraint, and replace it with the verified finding plus the project's own honest rationale.
Output: three corrected comment bodies, with zero change to any declaration, default, range, constant or line of executable code — each proven by a pre-recorded digest of that file's non-comment content.
</objective>
