<objective>
Add two diagonal-band themes whose background field is a tinted dithered field of the band's own
hue instead of White: a solid blue band on a light-blue field, and a solid red band on a light-red
field. Tone-on-tone.

The developer asked, verbatim: *"Je voudrais faire deux nouveaux thèmes à partir du thème diagonale.
1. Diagonale bleu et fond bleu claire. 2. Diagonale rouge et fond rouge clair."*

Purpose: extend the Phase 9 band family with the one combination it never covered — a coloured
field under the band. Every existing band theme sits on White, because spike 003 rendered every band
candidate against `build_canvas(theme_id="white")` and the band's colour was always a function
parameter, never a base-canvas property.

Output: two registry entries, extended test coverage that proves the dominance guard for real, and
four preview PNGs — then a blocking hand-off to the developer for on-glass confirmation.
</objective>

<investigation_findings>
Everything below was verified against the live code and by actually running a render during planning,
not reasoned about. Re-confirm anything you depend on; do not silently trust a number quoted here.

**F-1 — No render-engine change is needed. VERIFIED BY RENDERING.** During planning, two throwaway
tone-on-tone entries were injected into `device_config.THEMES` at runtime (no file edits) and rendered
through the real `render.build_canvas()`. All four renders (2 themes x 2 states) completed. This is
the whole basis of DP-4: `_build_active_canvas()` already composes the two features orthogonally and
in the correct order — it picks `dither.dithered_state_background(bg_idx)` when `theme_dithered()` is
true, else a flat `pf.new_canvas(bg_idx)`, and only then paints the band on top when
`theme_is_band()` is true. Background first, band over it. Two registry entries are genuinely
sufficient.

**F-2 — The dominance guard is helped, not stressed. MEASURED.** `_assert_legal_palette(canvas,
bg_idx)` asserts `bg_idx` is the single most common index. Because the band is the same hue as the
field, the band's pixels add to `bg_idx`'s own count instead of competing with it. Measured pixel
counts from the planning render:

| theme | state | bg_idx | bg pixels | next most common |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| band_blue_field | departing | Blue (4) | 1,349,782 | White 552,504 |
| band_blue_field | arriving | Blue (4) | 1,350,002 | White 552,284 |
| band_red_field | departing | Red (3) | 1,326,837 | White 551,653 |
| band_red_field | arriving | Red (3) | 1,327,079 | White 551,411 |

A ~2.4x margin in every case. Treat these as expected magnitudes, not as assertions to hardcode.

**F-3 — Ink is already handled downstream and must not be touched.** `draw_main_text_block()` sets
`effective_ink = IDX_WHITE` unconditionally for any band theme (Phase 9's 09-04 on-glass session
widened round 13's black-band-only override after real ink disagreed with the screen preview for
Blue, Green *and* Red). So the `ink_index` in a registry row does NOT colour the in-band text. It
colours everything OUTSIDE the band — the top labels, the previous-flight card, the battery icon,
the source-fault badge — all of which sit on the tinted field. That is exactly why DP-2 sources
`ink_index`/`weight` from the FIELD's precedent, not the band family's.

**F-4 — The overflow edge case changes shape but stays mitigated.** 09-04 recorded that white in-band
text overflowing the band lands on the plain White field and "silently vanishes (white-on-white)".
`draw_main_text_block()` already re-fits each line against the band's own width at its own y, which
mitigates it. On a tinted field the same overflow would land on light blue/light red instead — still
poor, but no longer invisible. This is NOT a reason to relax the existing re-fit. Do not change it.

**F-5 — The companion is already fully data-driven. VERIFIED.** `config_page.theme_fieldset()` loops
`device_config.THEME_IDS`; `theme_preview.py` has no per-theme branching and folds the theme's own
`THEMES` entry into `preview_signature()`, so new themes get preview chips with correctly-keyed
caches and no purge step. `companion/test_config_page.py` derives its counts from
`len(device_config.THEME_IDS)`. None of these need editing.

**F-6 — The one companion check that could genuinely have broken does not. VERIFIED.**
`test_companion_app.py`'s `_theme_preview_means_pairwise_distinct()` loops `THEME_IDS` and requires
every preview's mean RGB to be unique. Tone-on-tone previews sitting between `blue`/`blue_light`
were a real collision risk. Measured during planning: **18 themes, 18 unique means.**
`band_blue_field` lands at (119, 149, 184), between `blue` (78, 117, 165) and `blue_light`
(141, 166, 195); `band_red_field` at (183, 114, 116), clear of `red` (165, 70, 72), `red_light`
(193, 137, 139) and `band_red` (211, 180, 182). Comfortable margins, no code change required — but
re-run it, do not take this on trust.

**F-7 — Where the real work is: six hardcoded expected-maps in `server/test_config_history.py`.**
Unlike the companion, this harness pins exact id sets and fails closed on an unknown id. These are
the sites that WILL fail until extended: `_ink_contrast_pairing_is_correct`,
`_dithered_and_weight_contract_is_correct`, `_default_theme_and_labels_are_correct`,
`_theme_is_band_matches_registry_band_ids` (hardcodes the 5-id band set),
`_theme_band_index_matches_registry_or_none`, `_theme_band_dithered_matches_registry_or_false`.
`_theme_registry_shape_is_correct` is fully dynamic and needs no change.

**F-8 — `server/test_render.py` will not break, but its coverage is implicit.** Check #108
(`_band_themes_render_without_exception`) loops band themes dynamically in both states, so it picks
the new themes up for free. Check at the `len(non_band_ids) != 11` guard is safe: the new themes
carry `band_index`, so they are band themes and the non-band count stays 11. But #108 only proves
"no exception" — Task 2 adds the explicit dominance proof the developer asked for.

**F-9 — `EXPECTED_CHECK_COUNT` literals are shadowed in some files.** `companion/test_companion_app.py`
and `companion/test_config_page.py` each contain SEVERAL `EXPECTED_CHECK_COUNT` assignments from past
merges; the LAST one wins. Never grep for the first hit. Re-derive by RUNNING the harness (DP-5).
On-disk values read during planning, to be re-confirmed not trusted: `server/test_render.py` 127,
`server/test_config_history.py` 44.

**F-10 — Accepted cosmetic overlap, not a defect.** A chip's two `theme-chip__dot` swatches are drawn
from `departing_index`/`arriving_index`, so `blue`, `blue_light` and `band_blue_field` will all show
two identical blue dots. The rendered preview image is what actually distinguishes them (F-6), and it
does. Note it for the developer; do not "fix" it — that is a separate design question about the dot
idiom across the whole picker.
