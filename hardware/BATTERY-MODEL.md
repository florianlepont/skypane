## Method in plain words

Each time the frame wakes, it uses a fixed amount of charge. Between wakes it also draws a small steady amount while it sleeps. Two runs at two different cadences give two equations, and two equations separate those two unknowns.

Under the assumptions below (equal wake energy in both runs, which a later section questions), two things depend only on the counted wakes and the elapsed time: the ratio between the cost of one wake and the standing draw, and the ceiling (the days a frame would last if wakes cost nothing). The rated capacity (3000 mAh, rated, not measured) is only used to convert those into mAh per wake and mA.

Central fit (basis: raw cut, observed run-two polls, equal wake energy, equal usable charge):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Wake-to-standing ratio | 166.4626 s |
| Autonomy ceiling | 17.1127 days |
| Wake energy | 0.3378 mAh per wake (derived; capacity rated, not measured) |
| Standing draw | 7.3045 mA (derived; capacity rated, not measured) |

The standing figure is whatever consumption is not proportional to the number of wakes. It is not a measured hardware current. No comparison with any datasheet or typical current is made here. Whether it matches a real current is for the owner to verify with a measurement (see the Phase 47 recommendation below).

## Assumptions

- The same usable charge flows between the full-charge plateau and the first reading at or below 3300 mV in both runs.
- Wake energy is equal in both runs in the central fit; the envelope allows run one's wake to cost 1.0 to 2.0 times run two's because the newer firmware shortened the wake. Run two's wake costing more than run one's is not covered.
- Standing consumption is constant: it does not change with the interval, the temperature or the age of the pack.
- The awake overhead (observed mean gap minus configured interval) is measured per run from the data and added to the configured interval.
- The capacity is rated, not measured; it only converts the results to mAh and mA and does not move any autonomy figure.
- N counts observed polls in the window and T is the time from the first poll to the cut poll.
- Days are to the BATTERY EMPTY park, not to a dead pack: run one kept polling for about one more day, down to 2946 mV.

- Wake-energy ratio (run one against run two): 1.0, 1.5 or 2.0.

## What this model does not measure

- Image-download energy and display-refresh energy are not measured by the baseline hash-skip workload of either run.
- The share of wakes that download an image and refresh the panel is not recorded and changes with the interval, so every figure is an estimate to be confirmed on the real frame.

The interval you choose is therefore an estimate. It is to be confirmed on the real frame in Phase 47.

## Extrapolation warning

The two runs observed mean gaps between 74.0402 s (run two) and 327.8104 s (run one). Outside that range the model extrapolates instead of interpolating, so those rows are less trustworthy. Candidate intervals outside the observed range: 600 s, 900 s and 1800 s. The 300 s row is inside the range.

Quiet hours were off in run two. Production has them on, 23:30 to 06:00 (restored by the owner after the run, see `## Run 2 Run Conditions` in BATTERY-RUN.md). Also not modelled: the share of wakes that refresh the panel, temperature, and pack ageing.

## Six-month target (arithmetic by Claude, not tool output)

Owner goal, 2026-10-12: about six months between charges ("je cherche 6 mois"). This section is simple arithmetic on figures printed above; it is not produced by the tool and is not tested. It assumes the whole rated capacity is usable, which is optimistic, so the real requirement is stricter.

- Six months is about 180 days, or 4320 hours.
- Average current allowed for 180 days: 0.69 mA with 3000 mAh, 1.16 mA with 5000 mAh, 1.39 mA with 6000 mAh.
- Average current today at 300 s: about 11.2 mA (3000 mAh over the 11.18-day central figure).
- The central fit puts the standing draw at 7.3045 mA, which is above every allowed value in the list. In the central fit, even an interval with no wake cost would leave the 3000 mAh pack at the 17.11-day ceiling.
- The envelope also contains scenarios where the standing draw is close to zero (14 of 108 in the table above). The two runs cannot say which case is real.

What six months needs, in plain words: a bigger pack and a long interval, and, if the central fit is right, a much lower standing draw (roughly 1 mA or less). The model does not say what consumes the standing draw, and the section "Evidence from the parked phase" suggests there may be little of it; in that case the interval matters most.

The measurement that settles it: the frame's current while asleep, read with a meter in series with the battery (a range that reads milliamps down to microamps), with the display idle. One reading says whether the standing draw is near 7 mA, which would need hardware or firmware work, or near or below 1 mA, which makes six months plausible with a long interval. This extends the Phase 47 recommendation above.

Trade-off to accept: at six months the interval is likely an hour or more, which does not tell you whether you will catch the next train. The owner has said autonomy wins when the two conflict.

## Evidence from the parked phase (analysis by Claude, not tool output; 2026-10-22)

The central fit above says about 7.3 mA of the drain does not depend on how often the frame wakes. Data already in the repository points the other way. Nothing here is a measurement of current; it is arithmetic on the committed run-two export and on figures printed above, and it needs a real check.

1. **The parked phase barely discharged.** Run two ended its normal window at 2026-10-10T20:33:30 (3300 mV) and kept checking in hourly, parked, until 2026-10-12T16:22:02. After the first hours of rest (the voltage rose from 3296 to a peak of 3318 mV at 2026-10-11T00:34:20), it fell from 3318 mV to 3296 mV over 39.8 hours, 41 check-ins (40 intervals). With the repository's discharge curve (`server/device_policy.battery_fraction`, 6.23 % to 5.86 %) and the 3000 mAh rating, that is about 11 mAh, an average of about 0.28 mA over that span. If the 7.3 mA drain had been present, the pack would have lost about 290 mAh (about 10 %) in 39.8 hours, a fall of several hundred millivolts, not 22 mV.
2. **The two runs scale with awake time, not with a standing draw.** Like for like, both runs to the first reading at or below 3300 mV on the same 3000 mAh rating: run one is 3000/2992 = 1.0027 mAh per wake (arithmetic by Claude; the 0.923 mAh in `BATTERY-RUN.md` is the whole run to 2946 mV and is not like for like) with 27.8104 s awake, and run two is 0.48796 mAh per wake (3000/6148, the `mah_per_cycle` printed by `run-report` in `hardware/logs/battery-run2-report.json`) with 14.0402 s awake. The energy ratio is 2.055 and the awake-time ratio 1.981. The same figures give an average of about 129.8 mA while awake for run one and about 125.1 mA for run two. A fixed cost per awake second explains both runs without any standing draw.
3. **What this suggests.** The envelope's upper side (the cases where the standing draw is close to zero) may be more likely than the central fit; this rests on one parked span and is not established. In that case the cost of a wake is the whole story: about 0.488 mAh per wake with the current firmware.

Caveats: the voltage of a pack at rest creeps upward for hours, which hides part of the drain, so 0.28 mA is likely an underestimate; the curve and the 3000 mAh are ratings, not measurements; a parked frame may behave differently from a normal one; the average of 0.49 mA expected from the wakes alone (40 intervals of 0.48796 mAh over 39.8 hours) is higher than 0.28 mA, so the two are the same order of magnitude but do not match exactly.

### What it would mean for autonomy (arithmetic, only if point 3 holds)

With a 10000 mAh pack, an average current of 2.31 mA or less gives 180 days (10000 mAh over 4320 hours). With 0.48796 mAh per wake and 14.04 s of overhead per wake, the wake alone gives these averages: 300 s, about 5.6 mA (about 75 days); 600 s, about 2.9 mA (about 146 days); 900 s, about 1.9 mA (about 217 days); 1800 s, about 1.0 mA (about 430 days). Six months would then need an interval of roughly 12 to 14 minutes or more. Run two used one wake per 60 s plus 14 s overhead (23.7 mA, about 17.6 days), as above.

## Percentage calibration at the top of the curve (analysis by Claude, not tool output; 2026-10-28)

The owner noticed that in past data the battery never shows 100 %. This section records why, from the repository's own data and code. Nothing here is a measurement of the pack's voltage, and the curve in `server/device_policy.py` was not changed.

1. **What the data shows.** The first reading of run two is 4106 mV (2026-10-05T14:08:05), probably taken while the frame was still on the charger; the next readings are 4030 mV 96 s later and 4024 mV ten minutes later (`hardware/logs/battery-run2-export.jsonl`). The 900 s test started at 4058 mV (owner-reported, 2026-10-23; the time since unplugging was not recorded). On the repository's curve these read about 93 % (4030 mV) and 95 % (4058 mV).
2. **Where the 100 % knot comes from.** The top knot, 4112 mV, is the first reading of run one (2026-09-02 12:55), taken at the on-charger plateau of about 4120 mV (`hardware/BATTERY-RUN.md`, Discharge Trend). So 100 % on the curve means "reading while on the charger". A pack taken off the charger relaxes by tens of millivolts within minutes, so it cannot show 100 %. This is a choice of reference, not a calculation error.
3. **What is not established.** A Li-Po charged to 4.2 V normally rests near 4.15 to 4.2 V, and this pack rests near 4.03 to 4.06 V. Either the frame reads about 100 mV or more low, or the charger on the board stops below 4.2 V. The firmware only doubles the calibrated ADC reading (`firmware/main/battery_math.c`); there is no per-board trim. Only the pack's real voltage, read with a meter across its connector, can tell the two cases apart.
4. **What it touches.** The percentage shown to the owner is a few points too low at the top of the charge. The BATTERY EMPTY park is set in millivolts (3300 mV) and is not affected. Estimates that turn a voltage fall into charge through the curve, such as `logtools.py sleep-trend`, are sensitive to it: between the 3982 mV and 4000 mV knots the curve climbs from 79 % to 90 %, so a few millivolts of error there move the result a lot (the tool prints the sensitivity). A raw fall in millivolts per day does not depend on the curve and should be quoted next to any converted figure.
5. **Decision recorded.** Leave the curve unchanged for now. The replacement pack (10000 mAh) will have its own resting voltages, so the curve is to be corrected once, for the new pack, after the pack's resting voltage after a full charge and unplugging has been recorded and compared with a meter reading across its connector (optional comparison in `hardware/PACK-ACCEPTANCE.md`). Until then the percentage is a rough guide at the top of the charge.
