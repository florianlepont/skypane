## Method in plain words

Each time the frame wakes, it uses a fixed amount of charge. Between wakes it also draws a small steady amount while it sleeps. Two runs at two different cadences give two equations, and two equations separate those two unknowns.

Under the assumptions below, two things depend only on the counted wakes and the elapsed time: the ratio between the cost of one wake and the standing draw, and the ceiling (the days a frame would last if wakes cost nothing). The rated capacity (3000 mAh, rated, not measured) is only used to convert those into mAh per wake and mA.

Central fit (basis: raw cut, observed run-two polls, equal wake energy, equal usable charge):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Wake-to-standing ratio | 166.4626 s |
| Autonomy ceiling | 17.1127 days |
| Wake energy | 0.3378 mAh per wake (derived; capacity rated, not measured) |
| Standing draw | 7.3045 mA (derived; capacity rated, not measured) |

The standing figure is whatever consumption is not proportional to the number of wakes. It is not a measured hardware current. No comparison with any datasheet or typical current is made here. Whether it matches a real current is for the owner to verify with a measurement (see the Phase 47 recommendation below).

## Assumptions

- The same usable charge flows between the full-charge plateau and the first reading at or below 3300 mV in both runs.
- Wake energy is equal in both runs in the central fit; the envelope allows run one's wake to cost 1.0 to 2.0 times run two's because the newer firmware shortened the wake. Run two's wake costing more than run one's is not covered.
- Standing consumption is constant: it does not change with the interval, the temperature or the age of the pack.
- The awake overhead (observed mean gap minus configured interval) is measured per run from the data and added to the configured interval.
- The capacity is rated, not measured; it only converts the results to mAh and mA and does not move any autonomy figure.
- N counts observed polls in the window and T is the time from the first poll to the cut poll.
- Days are to the BATTERY EMPTY park, not to a dead pack: run one kept polling for about one more day, down to 2946 mV.

## Uncertainty

The tool does not give one number with a statistical interval. Two runs leave zero degrees of freedom, so no statistical interval is reported. Instead it recomputes the answer for 108 combinations of the choices that could reasonably differ, and the lowest and highest results are the minimum and maximum above. The axes:

- Run-one cut: raw, rolling median of 5 readings, rolling median of 49 readings.
- Run-two cycles: observed polls, or nominal cycles.
- Wake-energy ratio (run one against run two): 1.0, 1.5 or 2.0.
- Usable-charge ratio: -5 %, 0 % or +5 %.
- Overhead: from run two or from run one.

Scenario count: 108. Scenarios with no positive standing draw: 14.

Run two's continuity check is carried over, not hidden or retuned. Continuity verdict: FAIL.

- Coverage is 0.946 (observed=6148, nominal=6501.8), below the 0.95 gate.
- The largest gap between consecutive polls is 5.97 intervals, above the limit of 3.
- Run two's true cycle count lies between 6148 observed polls and 6501.8 nominal cycles. Both are carried in the envelope.
- No boot-counter witness exists for either run. Cycle counts are observed polls, not device boots.

Run one's raw cut is a single-sample dip. The readings around it, in mV, are 3366, 3372, 3368, 3292, 3368, 3372, 3366. Smoothing moves the cut later: the 5-reading median by 124 polls (0.4566 days), the 49-reading median by 147 polls (0.5471 days). The raw cut is the one used in the central fit, and the smoothed cuts are in the envelope.

## What this model does not measure

- Image-download energy and display-refresh energy are not measured by the baseline hash-skip workload of either run.
- The share of wakes that download an image and refresh the panel is not recorded and changes with the interval, so every figure is an estimate to be confirmed on the real frame.

The interval you choose is therefore an estimate. It is to be confirmed on the real frame in Phase 47.

## Extrapolation warning

The two runs observed mean gaps between 74.0402 s (run two) and 327.8104 s (run one). Outside that range the model extrapolates instead of interpolating, so those rows are less trustworthy. Candidate intervals outside the observed range: 600 s, 900 s and 1800 s. The 300 s row is inside the range.

## Not modelled: quiet hours and other field effects

Quiet hours were off in run two. Production has them on, 23:30 to 06:00 (restored by the owner after the run, see `## Run 2 Run Conditions` in BATTERY-RUN.md). During that window the frame sleeps until the window ends, so field autonomy should be somewhat longer than the table. This is kept as a note and not added to the table, because it would add an assumption nobody measured.

Also not modelled: the share of wakes that refresh the panel, temperature, and pack ageing.

## Applying the interval

The path a saved value takes: the companion Device settings save, then `device_config.json`, then the `sleep_s` in the reply to `GET /device/v1/display`. Two policies keep precedence over the saved value: the battery-critical park (3600 s) and the display-off cadence (300 s). Quiet hours only lengthen a wake, never shorten it.

Automated proof, in `stub-server/test_wake_interval_delivery.py`:

- `test_saved_interval_reaches_frame_sleep_s` (60, 300, 600, 900, 1800 and 3600 s).
- `test_unsaved_interval_falls_back_to_server_default` and `test_resaving_changes_the_next_reply`.
- `test_interval_save_carries_quiet_hours_and_display_forward`.
- `test_quiet_hours_never_shortens_saved_interval`.
- `test_safety_pins_win_over_saved_interval`.
- `test_wake_mirror_matches_frame_sleep_s` and `test_wake_mirror_matches_frame_without_saved_interval`.

`server/test_pipeline_e2e.py` also checks the exact value after recovery from the park.

One known difference, flagged in the plan 02 summary and not fixed: `server/wake.py` does not clamp a hand-edited out-of-range `wake_interval_s`, while the frame-facing path falls back to the server default. The companion form cannot save such a value, so this affects only hand-edited files and the companion's countdown.

Your procedure: open the live companion Device page, type the chosen value in seconds, save, reload, and read the stored value back. Confirming that the real frame obeys it belongs to Phase 47.

## Six-month target (arithmetic by Claude, not tool output)

Owner goal, 2026-10-12: about six months between charges ("je cherche 6 mois"). This section is simple arithmetic on figures printed above; it is not produced by the tool and is not tested. It assumes the whole rated capacity is usable, which is optimistic, so the real requirement is stricter.

- Six months is about 180 days, or 4320 hours.
- Average current allowed for 180 days: 0.69 mA with 3000 mAh, 1.16 mA with 5000 mAh, 1.39 mA with 6000 mAh.
- Average current today at 300 s: about 11.2 mA (3000 mAh over the 11.18-day central figure).
- The central fit puts the standing draw at 7.3045 mA, which is above every allowed value in the list. In the central fit, even an interval with no wake cost would leave the 3000 mAh pack at the 17.11-day ceiling.
- The envelope also contains scenarios where the standing draw is close to zero (14 of 108 in the table above). The two runs cannot say which case is real.

What six months needs, in plain words: a bigger pack, a long interval, and above all a much lower standing draw (roughly 1 mA or less). The model does not say what consumes the standing draw.

The measurement that settles it: the frame's current while asleep, read with a meter in series with the battery (a range that reads milliamps down to microamps), with the display idle. One reading says whether the standing draw is near 7 mA, which needs hardware or firmware work, or near or below 1 mA, which makes six months plausible. This extends the Phase 47 recommendation above.

Trade-off to accept: at six months the interval is likely an hour or more, which does not tell you whether you will catch the next train. The owner has said autonomy wins when the two conflict.
